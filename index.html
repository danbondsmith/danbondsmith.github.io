Daniela Bond-Smith

Research Economist, University of Hawaiʻi Economic Research Organization (UHERO)

I’m not a traditional academic. Most of my work is applied policy research. Whenever I can, I choose research topics for their practical relevance to people’s lives. My current research focuses on disability in Hawaiʻi, alongside other questions in health and economic policy.

I have a PhD in economics. I began my career in international economic policy in Europe and Australia. I then spent several years as a biostatistician and epidemiologist in academic and clinical settings. My expertise is quantitative data analysis and methods, especially survey data and administrative records. Since joining UHERO in 2021, I’ve had the opportunity to research disability, alongside other topics. This is the work I’m most passionate about. As an autistic person with chronic health conditions and a parent of children with disabilities, my work is motivated by my desire to contribute to the disability community.

I am a parent representative on the Hawaiʻi State Special Education Advisory Council. I’m also a member of the ALICE Research Advisory Council Hawaiʻi and the National ALICE Leadership Committee for People with Disabilities.

I welcome questions, ideas, and collaborations from families, community organizations, policymakers, and researchers. Please get in touch.
